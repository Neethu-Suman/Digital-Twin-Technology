# Digital Twin Classification III — Product, Process, and Performance

While the [integration-level classification](9.Digital_Twin_Classification:_Depending_on_the_Integration_Level.md) (Digital Model → Digital Shadow → Digital Twin) describes *how tightly* a twin is synchronized with its physical counterpart, this classification describes *what the twin is modeling* — i.e., its **purpose within the product lifecycle**. This framework (after Tao et al.) groups Digital Twins into three categories: **Product Twin**, **Process Twin**, and **Performance Twin**.

## The Three Categories

### 1. Product Twin
A **Product Twin** is a digital replica of a specific physical asset — its geometry, materials, physical properties, and behavior.

- Used primarily during **design and engineering**: virtual prototyping, structural/thermal/electromagnetic simulation, tolerance analysis.
- Focuses on **what the product is** — its structure and intrinsic properties — rather than how it's made or how it performs in the field.
- Typical examples: CAD-linked simulation models, a twin of a specific wearable sensor node used to validate its RF and mechanical design before fabrication.

### 2. Process Twin
A **Process Twin** models the **manufacturing or operational workflow** that produces or delivers the product — not the product itself.

- Used primarily during **production**: simulating assembly lines, production scheduling, resource allocation, throughput and bottleneck analysis.
- Focuses on **sequence, timing, and resource interaction** — how stations, machines, and materials come together.
- Typical examples: a twin of an assembly line simulating cycle times and queue lengths; a twin of a network deployment/rollout process.

### 3. Performance Twin
A **Performance Twin** focuses on the **operational, in-service behavior** of the product — how it actually performs once deployed.

- Used primarily during **operation and maintenance**: real-time monitoring, KPI tracking, degradation and fault analysis, predictive maintenance.
- Focuses on **behavior over time under real-world conditions** — the twin ingests live operational data (e.g., sensor telemetry) to track and forecast performance.
- Typical examples: a twin tracking a WBAN device's battery drain, link quality, and interference exposure over its deployed lifetime; a twin used for predictive maintenance of rotating machinery.

## Summary Table

| Category | Lifecycle Phase | Primary Question Answered | Typical Data Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Twin | Design / Engineering | What is the product, and does the design work? | CAD, simulation, material properties |
| Process Twin | Production / Manufacturing | How is the product made or deployed? | Manufacturing execution systems, scheduling data |
| Performance Twin | Operation / In-service | How does the product behave in real use? | Sensor telemetry, field monitoring, logs |

## How This Relates to the Integration-Level Classification

The two classifications are **orthogonal**: a Product, Process, or Performance twin can each be implemented at any integration level (Digital Model, Digital Shadow, Digital Twin, or Predictive Digital Twin). For example, a Performance Twin with real-time bi-directional sync and a forecasting layer would be both a "Performance Twin" (by purpose) and a "Predictive Digital Twin" (by integration level).
